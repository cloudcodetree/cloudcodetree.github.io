# NAM Player

A cross-platform app to play guitar through [Neural Amp Modeler](https://www.neuralampmodeler.com/) (NAM)
amp captures — with first-class support for the newer **A2 / A2-Lite** model
architecture and one-click tone downloads from [TONE3000](https://www.tone3000.com/).
Plug in your guitar, load an amp model (locally or straight from TONE3000's
catalog), and play, with a full practice-tone signal chain around it.

One C++/JUCE codebase targets Windows, macOS, Linux, iOS, and Android;
it is currently **desktop-verified end-to-end on macOS**.

## What it does

- **A2 neural amp models** — runs `.nam` captures (A1 and the newer A2
  "SlimmableContainer" format) via `mikeoliphant/NeuralAudio`.
- **Full signal chain**, each stage bypassable:
  `input gain → noise gate → amp model → speaker-cab IR → 3-band EQ → output gain`.
- **Speaker cab IRs** — loads `.wav` impulse responses through a direct-form FIR convolver.
- **Local library** — import, favorite, and recent lists for models and IRs, stored offline.
- **TONE3000 download** — OAuth2 PKCE against TONE3000's hosted picker; a chosen
  tone's A2 model lands directly in the library.
- **Low-latency audio** — native backends per OS (CoreAudio / ASIO / ALSA / Oboe),
  a device/buffer picker, live latency readout, and a lock-free level/CPU/xrun meter.

## The part I care most about

The codebase splits into a **JUCE-free, headlessly unit-tested core** (DSP,
model/library, networking — ~70 test cases) and a thin JUCE shell. The #1
invariant: **the real-time audio callback never allocates, locks, throws, or
does I/O.** Model and IR hand-offs use a lock-free atomic-pointer pattern with
control-thread-owned lifetimes. Keeping the hard parts JUCE-free makes them
portable — a WebAssembly build is a realistic future target.
